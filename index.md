In April 1919, a dental surgeon named Mani Ram ran to Jallianwala Bagh, the open ground in the middle of Amritsar where his son Madan Mohan liked to play. The boy had not come home. Mani Ram found him among the dead and carried his body home. Two years later, he had to tell a British committee what his son's life was worth.

## What happened on 13 April 1919

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**✏️ Write this section.** Facts you can use:

- It was Baisakhi; the crowd included children (Dhillon).
- Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer had troops close off the Bagh and fire without warning; 1,650 rounds were fired (Dhillon).
- Colonial records list 376 people killed (Pal, *The Tribune*).
- It was part of a wider 1919 crackdown on protests against the Rowlatt Act: martial law, floggings, and bombing from planes in Gujranwala (Dhillon).

## Britain paid, but quietly, unequally, and without regret

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**✏️ Write this section.** This is the core of your argument. Facts you can use:

- Rs 523,571 went to Europeans harmed in the 1919 unrest, paid from fines and indemnities imposed on Indians (Dhillon).
- By February 1921, Indian victims' families had received only small "confidential" payments of Rs 200–600 (Pal; Dhillon).
- After Indian legislators pushed in 1921, a committee paid out about Rs 22.66 lakh by June 1922 (Pal).
- Lives were priced by income: a few thousand rupees for daily wage earners, up to Rs 1.01 lakh for a businessman. Only 218 of the 376 families of those killed were compensated (Pal).
- Indian women and children were at first left out of the formula (Dhillon).
- Officials refused any statement of regret (Dhillon).
</div>

## Why money without an apology isn't redress

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**✏️ Write this section.** Bring in your course readings and ethical theories:

- **Lazare**, Ch. 3: what a real apology needs (acknowledging the offense and accepting responsibility).
- **Duty (Hinman, Ch. 6)**: pricing Indian lives far below European ones failed to treat people as ends in themselves.
- **Justice (Hinman, Ch. 8)**: unjust in who got paid and in how it was decided.
- **Civil Liberties Act of 1988**: the U.S. paired an apology *with* payment for Japanese American incarceration. Britain did the reverse.
